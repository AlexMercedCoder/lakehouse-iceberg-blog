## The Two Giants: ChatGPT and Claude

OpenAI launched ChatGPT in November 2022 and sparked the current AI boom. Anthropic launched Claude shortly after, positioning it as a safety focused alternative. In 2026, both companies have mature products with overlapping features and distinct strengths.

The choice between them is not about which one is "better." It is about which one fits your specific needs. They are more similar than different at the basic level, but their advanced features diverge significantly.

---

## ChatGPT: Features and Pricing

### Free Tier

ChatGPT's free tier gives you unlimited text chats with GPT-6 (subject to OpenAI's abuse guardrails), plus limited file uploads, image creation, voice chats, deep research, memory, and Codex access. The limits on tools and uploads are where free users feel the squeeze.

You can use it for simple tasks: answering questions, drafting short text, basic brainstorming. But the free tier is designed to give you a taste, not a full experience. Heavy users hit the tool and upload limits quickly and consider upgrading.

### ChatGPT Go: $8 per Month

Go sits between Free and Plus. It adds more messages with tools, more uploads, more image creation, more voice chats, and longer memory. OpenAI notes that this plan may include ads. If you mostly chat and only occasionally hit the free limits, Go is the cheapest way to raise them.

### ChatGPT Plus: $20 per Month

The $20 tier is where ChatGPT becomes genuinely useful. Here is what you get.

**Access to GPT-6 reasoning models.** Plus unlocks OpenAI's advanced reasoning models in the GPT-6 family, including GPT-6.1 Sol, GPT-6 Astra, and GPT-6 Luna, with usage limits. The reasoning context window is 256K tokens on Plus, roughly 320 pages of input according to OpenAI's pricing page.

The practical difference between the models is noticeable. GPT-6 Astra is OpenAI's most capable model for complex reasoning and coding, while Luna is built for fast, focused tasks. For quick questions and simple tasks, the lighter models perform almost as well, so save your heavier reasoning for the hardest problems.

**Voice conversations.** Voice mode on the mobile app supports real time conversation. You can interrupt it mid sentence, and the latency is low enough that the conversation feels natural.

The voice mode has become a primary interface for many users. They dictate emails, brainstorm ideas verbally, practice presentations, and have the model read documents aloud. It is surprisingly effective for tasks where typing is inconvenient.

**Image generation.** Plus includes more complex and accurate image creation than the free tier. You can generate images from text descriptions, edit existing images, and create variations. The quality is competitive with dedicated image tools for many use cases, though specialized tools like Midjourney still have fans for artistic work.

The image generation is deeply integrated into the chat interface. You can generate an image, discuss it with the model, request changes, and iterate without switching applications. This tight feedback loop is more efficient than using separate tools for conversation and image generation.

**File uploads and data analysis.** Upload PDFs, Word documents, Excel spreadsheets, and images. ChatGPT reads the content and can analyze it. You can ask it to find patterns in a spreadsheet, summarize a long PDF, or extract data from images. This feature works well enough for everyday analysis but hits limits with very large files or complex multi sheet workbooks.

One underappreciated use: upload a messy CSV file and ask ChatGPT to clean it. "Remove duplicate rows. Standardize the date column to ISO format. Flag any rows with missing values." ChatGPT processes the data and gives you a cleaned version. For non technical users, this replaces a dozen spreadsheet formulas.

**Web browsing.** ChatGPT can search the internet for current information when you enable the browsing feature. It cites its sources. This is useful for research that requires up to date information, though the browsing is slower than using a search engine directly because the model needs to search, read, and synthesize in sequence.

**Projects, scheduled tasks, and custom GPTs.** Plus includes Projects for grouping chats and files, scheduled tasks, and custom GPTs: your own specialized versions of ChatGPT with custom instructions, knowledge files, and configured capabilities. The GPT Store offers community created GPTs for specific tasks: writing assistant, code reviewer, travel planner, fitness coach. Many are shallow wrappers, but a well made custom GPT can save significant setup time for recurring tasks.

The best use of Custom GPTs is creating one for your own recurring workflows. A "Meeting Notes GPT" that always formats output the same way. A "Content Repurposer" that turns any document into social posts, a newsletter summary, and a blog outline. The time saved from not repeating instructions adds up.

**Memory.** ChatGPT Plus includes persistent memory across sessions. You can tell the model facts about yourself once, and it remembers them in future conversations. "I prefer concise answers." "I work in healthcare compliance." "My team has seven people." These details persist and shape future responses without you repeating them.

### ChatGPT Pro: From $100 per Month

Pro comes in three usage tiers starting at $100 per month. It adds Pro reasoning powered by GPT-6 Astra, longer Codex sessions, unlimited and faster image creation, maximum deep research, memory, and context (a 400K token reasoning window), and Dot, OpenAI's always on agent. This plan is for power users who depend on ChatGPT for their daily work and hit the Plus limits regularly.

The main question to ask yourself: are you hitting the Plus limits more than once a week? If yes, Pro might be worth it. If no, save your money.

## Claude: Features and Pricing

### Claude Free

Claude's free tier gives you Claude Sonnet and Haiku with usage limits on a rolling five hour window. It includes memory and up to five Projects, and you can use it for text chat, file uploads (PDFs, images, documents), and basic analysis. The free tier works for light use: quick questions, document summaries, short writing tasks.

### Claude Pro: $20 per Month ($17 Billed Annually)

The Pro tier adds Claude Opus, Claude Code, and at least five times the Free plan's usage per session. Here is what you get.

**Claude Opus access.** Claude Opus 5.5 is the model Anthropic recommends for most work, and Claude Fable 5.1 sits above it for the most demanding reasoning (on Pro, Fable draws on usage credits). Opus excels at reasoning, writing, and analysis. Many users report that Claude produces more naturally flowing, better structured long form writing than ChatGPT. The difference is subjective and task dependent, but it is a real distinction.

**Up to 1M token context window.** Claude's context window has been a differentiator since its early days. Current Claude models accept up to 1 million tokens, which Anthropic estimates at roughly 555,000 words, though the window available in the app varies by model. You can paste several books into a single conversation and ask questions about them. This is useful for analyzing large documents, comparing multiple files, or working with long codebases.

**Projects.** Projects let you organize conversations, documents, and custom instructions into dedicated workspaces. Each project can have its own knowledge base (uploaded documents), custom instructions, and conversation history. This is useful for ongoing work on a specific topic: a project for your book research, another for your job search, another for your side project. Pro includes unlimited Projects. ChatGPT now offers Projects too, but Claude's version remains one of its strongest organizational tools.

**Claude Mobile App.** Full featured mobile app with voice input, image upload, and conversation sync across devices. The mobile experience is similar to ChatGPT's.

**Claude Code.** Claude's coding assistant runs in your terminal, desktop app, and IDE. It can read and write files, execute commands, and manage entire codebases. It can create files, run your project's test suite, and fix errors automatically.

**Research and Claude in Chrome.** Pro adds Research for multi source investigations and Claude in Chrome, a browser extension that lets Claude work inside web pages for you.

**Cowork and computer use.** Anthropic is rolling Cowork, its desktop agent for knowledge work, out to Pro and Max. Computer use, where Claude operates your apps by clicking and typing, is a research preview on both plans. Both are described in the Max section below because heavy agent work burns through usage limits fast.

### Claude Max: $100 or $200 per Month

Claude Max is Anthropic's top individual tier. Max 5x costs $100 per month and Max 20x costs $200. Both add higher output limits, early access to new features, priority access during busy periods, and monthly API credits. Here is what the extra usage buys you.

**5x to 20x higher usage limits.** Max gives you five or twenty times the Pro plan's usage per session, with a weekly limit across all models. For heavy daily users, this is the main reason to upgrade. Instead of rationing your messages, you use Claude freely throughout the day, including long agent tasks in Cowork and Claude Code.

**Claude Cowork.** This is the desktop agent feature, built on the same agent architecture as Claude Code. Cowork runs in the Claude Desktop app and can read and write files on your computer, use connected services, and perform multi step tasks. It is like having an assistant that can actually use your computer.

The practical difference from a regular chatbot is large. Normal chatbots can only process text you give them. Cowork reaches your work directly. It tries connectors such as Gmail, Google Drive, Microsoft 365, or Slack first, then a browser, and only then falls back to operating your screen. "Which Slack message from the engineering channel is unresolved?" becomes a question it can answer by reading the channel itself.

Cowork can also take actions. "Open my analytics dashboard, take a screenshot of the weekly active users chart, and save it to the Desktop." Claude opens the browser, loads the page, captures the screenshot, and saves it, asking your permission before it touches a new app.

**Dispatch.** Dispatch connects the Claude mobile app to the Claude Desktop app. You send a task from your phone, and Claude executes it on your desktop computer. The desktop does the actual work: opening files, running commands, interacting with applications. Your phone just provides the instructions and receives the results. As of October 2026, Anthropic lists Dispatch as a limited beta for some Pro and Max plans and says it is not available to new users; cloud Cowork sessions are the suggested alternative.

Dispatch is not a remote desktop. You do not see your desktop screen on your phone. You send a text instruction, and Claude sends back the results. It is asynchronous task delegation. You can check on progress from your phone, ask follow up questions, or send additional instructions to the same session.

**Computer use.** Claude can control your mouse and keyboard to interact with software just like a human would. It can open your browser, navigate to a website, and fill out a form. It can open your spreadsheet, enter data, and save the file. You turn it on in the desktop app under Settings, General, and Claude asks permission before it accesses each app. Anthropic advises against using it with financial accounts, legal or medical documents, or apps that hold other people's personal data.

Computer Use works through screenshots and coordinate based clicking. Claude sees what is on your screen, decides what to click, and simulates the mouse movement and click. It is slower than a human for simple tasks but much faster for repetitive data entry or multi step workflows that require switching between applications.

The current limitations: Computer Use can struggle with applications that have unusual interfaces, custom UI components, or pages that render differently on different screen sizes. It works best with standard web applications and common desktop software.

**Skills and scheduled tasks.** Skills are reusable workflows with custom instructions, and Claude can run tasks on a schedule. You define a workflow once and trigger it on demand or on a timer.

Common examples: "Every morning, check my inbox for emails from my direct reports, summarize any requests, and save the summary to my Desktop." "Before each client meeting, pull the account history from the CRM, open the latest proposal, and compile a briefing document." "Every Friday at 4 PM, check my task list for overdue items and report on status."

**Priority access.** New features often arrive first for Max subscribers, and Max users get priority access during high traffic periods. For time sensitive work, this matters.

## Desktop Apps: Beyond the Web Chat Interface

Both ChatGPT and Claude have desktop applications that go beyond the web interface. This is where the real productivity gains live.

### ChatGPT Desktop App

The ChatGPT desktop app (available for macOS and Windows) provides a persistent chat window that stays on top of your work. Key features include:

**Voice mode.** Hands free conversation with the model. You can dictate prompts and hear responses spoken aloud. Useful when cooking, driving, or doing tasks that occupy your hands.

**Screen capture.** Take a screenshot of anything on your screen and send it directly to the chat. ChatGPT analyzes the image and responds. Use this for: "Explain this error message," "Turn this chart into a table," "Read this article and summarize it."

**Work with other apps.** On macOS, the desktop app can read from supported apps such as code editors and terminals, and it can make code edits directly. Plus and Pro also include ChatGPT Work for longer tasks on desktop, web, and mobile.

**File access.** Open local files from your computer directly in the chat. No need to upload through a web interface.

### Claude Desktop App

The Claude desktop app goes further than a chat window because of the Cowork and Dispatch features. Key features include:

**Cowork mode.** Claude sees your screen and can interact with your applications. You can say "Open the spreadsheet in my Downloads folder, find the row where revenue dropped more than 10%, and explain what the data shows about that month." Claude opens the file, reads it, finds the relevant data, and gives you an analysis.

**Skills and scheduled tasks.** Save a workflow as a skill, and Claude can run it on demand or on a schedule. Common examples include: "Summarize my morning emails," "Create a meeting brief from the last three Slack messages and the calendar event," and "Find all unpaid invoices from last month and compile them into a report."

**Dispatch remote control.** Here is how it works in practice, for users who have access to the beta. You are on the train commuting to work. You open the Claude mobile app and type: "For my 9 AM meeting, find the proposal draft in my Google Drive, check if the pricing section was updated, and put the latest version on my desktop so I can review it when I arrive." Dispatch sends this task to your desktop at home or office. Claude finds the file, checks the version history, and places the document on your desktop. When you arrive, everything is ready.

Dispatch works for longer running tasks too. Start a research project from your phone while waiting in line. Dispatch triggers Claude to start the work on your desktop. By the time you sit down, the research is done and the results are waiting.

**The always on laptop requirement.** Dispatch requires your computer to be awake and running the Claude Desktop app. If your computer goes to sleep, the work stops. Users typically set their computer to never sleep when they plan to use Dispatch remotely. Closing your laptop at the end of the day means Dispatch stops working. Using a desktop Mac mini or keeping a work laptop powered on solves this, but it adds a power and security consideration. If you need work to continue while your computer is off, use a cloud Cowork session instead.

## Practical Daily Productivity Examples

Here are specific workflows using the paid features of ChatGPT and Claude.

### Email Management (Claude Cowork With Dispatch)

Set up a scheduled Cowork task that runs every morning at 7 AM. Claude reads the last 24 hours of messages through your email connector and summarizes them into a briefing document on your desktop. While you commute, check the summary on your phone. If you have Dispatch, send follow ups: "Reply to Sarah confirming the meeting time" or "Save the attached contract to the Projects folder."

The time savings compound. A single email triage session that used to take 15 minutes now takes 2 minutes. Over a month, that is over four hours saved.

### Meeting Preparation (ChatGPT Plus or Pro)

Before a meeting, gather the relevant materials: the calendar invite, the previous meeting notes, any documents shared in the thread. Drag them all into the ChatGPT desktop app. Ask: "Summarize the key decisions from our last meeting. List the action items that were due today. Based on this agenda, what are the three most important topics we need to discuss?"

The chat persists, so you can follow up during the meeting: "Take notes on this discussion and compare them to the agenda." After the meeting: "Draft the meeting summary email based on our discussion."

### Document Comparison (Claude Pro and Its Large Context Window)

You have three vendor proposals, each 40 pages. Upload all three to a single Claude Pro chat. Ask: "Compare these three proposals on pricing, delivery timeline, warranty terms, and cancellation policy. Create a comparison table ranked by total cost of ownership over three years."

Claude's context window handles all three documents in a single conversation. You can ask follow ups about specific sections: "In the second proposal, what is the penalty for late delivery?" "Which proposal has the most favorable payment terms?"

### Research and Content Drafting (Either Service, Both Work)

Use Claude Pro's Projects or ChatGPT's Custom GPTs to set up a dedicated workspace for a writing project. Upload your research documents, outline, and style guide. Each time you sit down to write, the project context is already loaded. You do not need to re explain your requirements.

For long form content, use Claude's Projects with custom instructions. Set the instructions to match your writing style: "Write in short paragraphs. Use active voice. Start with a concrete example. Avoid listing features without explaining the benefit." The project remembers these instructions across sessions.

### Personal Knowledge Management (ChatGPT Memory)

Use ChatGPT's Memory feature to build a persistent knowledge base about your work and life. Save facts once and reference them later. Tell the model your preferred communication style, the names of your team members, the projects you are working on, and your weekly schedule.

Over time, ChatGPT builds a profile that makes its responses more relevant to your specific situation. When you ask for meeting agenda suggestions, it knows who attends your meetings and what topics are active. When you ask for help drafting an email, it knows the recipient's context from previous conversations.

The tradeoff is privacy. The memory persists across sessions, which means you are trusting OpenAI with personal and professional information. If that makes you uncomfortable, disable the memory feature and use a manual approach instead.

### Expense and Receipt Processing (Claude Pro or Max Using Computer Use)

Take a photo of a stack of receipts and send it to Claude. Claude's desktop agent opens your expense tracking spreadsheet, reads each receipt, categorizes the expenses, and enters the data. You review the results before anything is submitted. Keep this to your own spreadsheet: Anthropic advises against pointing computer use at financial accounts.

This workflow turns a tedious 20 minute weekly task into a 30 second photo and approval step. The accuracy depends on receipt quality and handwriting legibility. Blurry or folded receipts may need manual correction.

### Data Extraction from Screenshots (ChatGPT Desktop)

Take a screenshot of a table in a PDF that does not allow copy paste. Drag the screenshot into the ChatGPT desktop app. Ask: "Convert this table into a CSV format I can paste into Excel." ChatGPT reads the image, extracts the data, and formats it correctly. This works for printed tables, screenshots of dashboards, and images of documents.

### Schedule Management (Claude Pro With a Calendar Connector)

Connect your calendar to Claude to plan your day. "What does my calendar look like this week? Identify the three most important meetings and suggest preparation steps for each. Flag any scheduling conflicts." Claude reads your calendar, analyzes the events, and gives you a structured overview.

For recurring meetings, set up a Project that contains the meeting context, attendee list, and agenda template. Before each meeting, ask Claude to review the previous meeting's notes from the project and draft the agenda for the current one.

### Code Assistance (Claude Code or ChatGPT)

Both services handle code well. ChatGPT's chat interface works when you paste code and ask for changes, and its Codex agent can work on a whole repository. Claude Code runs in your terminal, desktop app, or IDE and makes changes directly to your files.

For non developers, Claude Code is less relevant. But ChatGPT's code features still help: ask it to write a formula for your spreadsheet, create a simple script to rename files, or explain what a line of code in a document does.

### Voice to Document (Claude With Dispatch)

Use Claude's voice input on your phone to dictate a rough draft of a document while walking or commuting. With Dispatch, Claude on your desktop formats it into a proper document with sections and headers. When you get to your desk, the draft is ready to edit.

This workflow is particularly good for first drafts. Dictating removes the friction of staring at a blank page. The content will be rough, but rough content is much easier to edit than to create from nothing.

## Which One Should You Choose

The honest answer depends on what you need.

**Choose ChatGPT Plus ($20) if you want:** The broadest set of features at the standard price point, including image creation, deep research, Codex, Projects, and the GPT Store. ChatGPT's ecosystem is larger than Claude's, with more community created tools. If you want one service that does a bit of everything, ChatGPT is the safe pick. If you only need higher chat limits, Go at $8 may be enough.

**Choose Claude Pro ($20) if you want:** Better long form writing, a context window large enough for several long documents at once, Projects, Claude Code, and Cowork as it rolls out. Claude tends to produce more natural prose with less prompting, which matters if you do a lot of writing.

**Choose ChatGPT Pro (from $100) if:** You depend on ChatGPT for daily work and consistently hit the Plus limits. You want Pro reasoning on GPT-6 Astra, longer Codex sessions, and the maximum deep research allowance.

**Choose Claude Max ($100 or $200) if:** You run long Cowork, computer use, or Claude Code sessions every day and keep hitting the Pro limits. Max does not unlock a different set of agent features so much as give you room to use them all day. If you work across multiple devices and have Dispatch access, Max makes that workflow practical.

**Get both if:** You have a budget for it and the tasks justify the cost. Many power users maintain subscriptions to both. They use ChatGPT for quick queries, image creation, and web research. They use Claude for writing, document analysis, and agent tasks on the desktop. The combined $40 a month at the standard tiers covers almost every AI use case.

But the honest truth is that most people do not need to pay $100 or more a month for either service. Start with one $20 subscription. Use it for a month. If you hit limits or want features you do not have, consider upgrading or adding the second service.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is ChatGPT Plus or Claude Pro better?

Both cost $20 a month. ChatGPT Plus is the broader all rounder, with image creation, custom GPTs, Codex, and deep research. Claude Pro is stronger for long form writing and large documents, and it includes Claude Code, Claude in Chrome, and Cowork as it rolls out.

### How much do ChatGPT and Claude cost in 2026?

ChatGPT has Free, Go at $8, Plus at $20, and Pro from $100 per month with three usage tiers. Claude has Free, Pro at $20 per month ($17 a month billed annually), and Max at $100 for 5x Pro usage or $200 for 20x.

### Which models do ChatGPT and Claude use?

As of October 2026, ChatGPT runs on OpenAI's GPT-6 family, with Pro reasoning powered by GPT-6 Astra. Claude Pro and Max include Claude Opus 5.5, Sonnet 5.5, and Haiku 5.5, and Claude Fable 5.1 is available for the hardest reasoning work through usage credits or Max limits.

### Do I need Claude Max for Cowork or Dispatch?

No. Anthropic is rolling Cowork out to both Pro and Max, and computer use is a research preview on both plans. Dispatch, which sends tasks from your phone to your desktop, is a limited beta on some Pro and Max plans and is not open to new users.

### Does ChatGPT train on my conversations?

On consumer plans, OpenAI's pricing page says your content is used to train its models, with an opt out available in settings. If that matters for your work, turn it off or use a business plan.
